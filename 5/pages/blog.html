Study Guide 01

Diagram first.

By Priya Sharma · Senior Physics Faculty

Before selecting any mathematical formula, always sketch the physical system. Mark what is in motion, what is fixed, and every force vector or physical quantity you know. A simple diagram reduces the cognitive load on working memory and makes the next step immediately visible.

  1. Sketch the system: Use simple geometric blocks, planes, and vectors.
  2. Label known parameters: Note values, signs, and standard SI units.
  3. Identify the target unknown: Explicitly mark the variable to solve.
  4. Select the core principle: Choose the fundamental formula that connects knowns and unknowns.

This single habit eliminates over 70% of common algebraic and sign errors on exam day.

Study Guide 02

Read for claims.

By James Lewis · Lead English Faculty

Close analytical reading is not about coloring every sentence. Read a paragraph once for narrative flow, then pause and ask three precise questions:

  • What central claim is the author making in this paragraph?
  • Which specific textual detail or word choice directly supports it?
  • What shift in tone occurs between the start and the conclusion?

Limit yourself to one brief margin note per paragraph. If you cannot summarize the idea in a short phrase, re-read before adding more commentary.

Study Guide 03

Plan by evidence.

By Northstar Academic Advisory Team

Place your exam date at the end of the calendar and work strictly backward. Allocate the most weekly time to concepts you cannot yet explain or solve without referring to notes.

  1. Group all topics: Categorize syllabus into Secure (green), Unsure (yellow), and Weak (red).
  2. Target yellow & red first: Dedicate the earliest, freshest study hours to weak areas.
  3. End with active recall: Close the session with 3 timed questions answered strictly from memory.
  4. Reserve final days for mixed sets: Practice full timed papers rather than first-time reading.
